# Build a livescore site for under $20

A Ugandan student can build a real, actively updating four-sport livescore site, but only football has a credible near-zero-budget path today, and tennis is an unsolved hole. The best-supported stack is **API-Football (API-Sports) for football, the separate API-Sports basketball and volleyball products if they are confirmed, a Cloudflare Cron Worker that polls once per minute and writes JSON to R2, and a static front end that polls a CDN-cached JSON file every 20-30 seconds**. The prototype costs **$0 per month**. Football growth scale costs about **$19 per month** for API-Football Pro (7,500 requests/day), and a three-sport version plausibly lands at $50-80 per month. That range is my estimate, because the volleyball price is unknown. Tennis is the budget breaker: the only well-documented licensed feed, Goalserve, costs **$150 per month** and the cheap alternatives are scrapers or vendors that bar public republishing. The legal position is the biggest unknown: no provider's terms of service could be read, so permission for public, ad-supported display is unconfirmed everywhere. Scraping Flashscore or Sofascore is not a safe shortcut. Several official pages (api-sports.io pricing, football-data.org, Sportmonks pricing, api-tennis.com, balldontlie) were blocked in research, so most prices below come from provider blog posts and competitor comparisons. Coverage of the Uganda Premier League and CAF competitions is not confirmed for any provider, and that is the first thing to test.

## One cheap vendor covers football, and everything else needs a test

API-Football is the best fit for a zero-budget football start. Its plans are Free at 100 requests/day, Pro at **$19** for 7,500/day, Ultra at $29 for 75,000/day and Mega at $39 for 150,000/day. The pricing page says every plan includes all competitions and endpoints, and the plans are prepaid with no overage charges ([API-Football pricing](https://www.api-football.com/pricing)). Live fixtures and events refresh every 15 seconds, so the data can be polled but not pushed ([API-Football guide](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-to-get-started-with-api-football-the-complete-beginners-guide)). The alternatives are weaker for this builder. football-data.org's free tier is delayed and has no African competitions among its 12 ([TheStatsAPI](https://thestatsapi.com/blog/free-football-api-alternatives)). Sportmonks starts at EUR 29 for five leagues, and its free plan is two leagues in Denmark and Scotland ([TheStatsAPI](https://thestatsapi.com/blog/best-football-api)). Goalserve's football live-score plan is $150 per month ([Goalserve prices](https://goalserve.com/es/sport-data-feeds/soccer-api/prices)).

For the other three sports the evidence is thinner, and the picture is mixed. For basketball, secondary reports price API-Sports basketball at roughly $15-35 per month across tiers. One source says each sport is billed as a separate subscription, which conflicts with the "one key, shared quota" idea ([Highlightly](https://highlightly.net/blogs/best-nba-apis-in-2026); [api.market](https://api.market/blog/recodex/allsportsapi/best-sports-api)). For NBA only, balldontlie's free tier gives 5 requests per minute for teams, players and games ([Highlightly](https://highlightly.net/blogs/best-nba-apis-in-2026)). For volleyball, the API-Sports volleyball product is something I believe exists from prior knowledge, but no source in the notes confirms its limits, price or fields. Sportradar covers only FIVB and CEV indoor tournaments, and domestic leagues such as Italy and Poland are not mentioned ([Sportradar volleyball docs](https://docs.sportradar.com/volleyball)). Tennis is the real gap, because no API-Sports tennis product could be confirmed. Goalserve documents ATP, WTA, Challenger and ITF live scores with 5-second point-by-point updates at **$150 per month** and a 30-day free trial ([Goalserve tennis prices](https://goalserve.com/en/sport-data-feeds/tennis-api/prices)). Apify actors that appear to scrape Flashscore charge about $4 per 1,000 matches, plus a per-run fee that makes minute-by-minute polling cost roughly $2.88 per day before match fees ([Apify](https://apify.com/tagadanar/tennis-live-scores)).

One tempting all-in-one option should be rejected. SportsAPIPro covers 25+ sports under one key and costs $45, $89 or $179 per month for its paid tiers, but its data-rights page bars publicly republishing the data ([SportsAPIPro rate limits](https://docs.sportsapipro.com/rate-limits.md); [data rights](https://docs.sportsapipro.com/data-rights)). That rules it out for a public site. It is easily confused with API-Sports, which is a different company. My recommended combination is therefore as follows.

| Sport | Primary pick | Cost | Status in the notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Football | API-Football Free for development, Pro for launch | $0, then $19/mo | Pricing confirmed on the official page; Uganda and CAF coverage unverified |
| Basketball | API-Sports basketball (NBA-only fallback: balldontlie free) | about $15-35/mo, secondary sources | Unconfirmed on official pages |
| Volleyball | API-Sports volleyball | unknown | Existence and coverage unconfirmed |
| Tennis | Goalserve tennis (test on the 30-day trial) or defer | $150/mo | Documented by vendor; too costly for zero budget |

## Poll once, cache everywhere, and visitors cost nothing upstream

The core design is a central poller, a shared store and small cacheable JSON endpoints, so provider requests scale with the number of sports and not with visitors. Sofascore reports a 97-99% CDN cache hit rate and Livesport runs scouts plus an owned B2B feed, so a small team should copy the cache-first pattern and skip the scouts ([Fastly case study](https://www.fastly.com/es/customers/sofascore); [Seznam Zprávy](https://www.seznamzpravy.cz/clanek/ekonomika-b2b-sto-milionu-lidi-chodi-pro-sportovni-vysledky-do-ceska-vse-se-ridi-z-prahy-215993)). The request math is simple. Four sports polled once a minute for 24 hours is 5,760 requests per day, which fits Pro's 7,500. Polling every 30 seconds in a 12-hour window is the same 5,760. Polling every 15 seconds around the clock is 23,040, which needs Ultra at 75,000. The free tier's 100 requests per day is only enough for development and parser testing. Add 20-50% for fixtures-by-date and match-detail calls (my estimate), and poll only sports that have matches live or starting soon.

On Cloudflare the poller is a Cron Worker, which runs at most once per minute and has 10 ms of CPU time on the free plan ([Cron Triggers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/cron-triggers/); [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). It holds the provider key as a secret, calls each provider's bulk live endpoint (for API-Football, `fixtures?live=all`), normalizes the result and writes `scores.json` to R2. R2 is the right store because KV's free tier allows only 1,000 writes per day, below the 1,440 that one write per minute needs, while R2 allows 1M writes per month and has free egress ([KV pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/platform/pricing/); [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). R2 is served through a custom domain with a Cache Everything rule at a 15-30 second edge TTL, so visitor hits never invoke a Worker. The `r2.dev` URL is rate-limited and for development only, so a domain is required ([R2 public buckets](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/public-buckets/)). The domain's price is not in the notes. The endpoint set can stay small: one day-scoreboard file per UTC date, one live-now file, and one file per match for events. Each file carries a `last_updated` timestamp.

The front end should poll, not use SSE or WebSockets. Polling a cached file costs one cache hit per interval with no long-lived connection, whereas SSE defeats edge caching and needs a Durable Object or a VPS to hold sockets. The browser polls every 20-30 seconds, pauses when the tab is hidden and uses ETag and `If-None-Match` so unchanged polls return 304. Times travel as UTC and render in `Africa/Kampala` (UTC+3). These engineering choices are my judgement, not sourced findings. Build the internal data model around a normalized `state` (scheduled, live, break, finished, postponed and so on) with provider status codes stored alongside, so adding a second provider for tennis later does not touch the UI. Cloudflare also has Mombasa and Kampala presence, and its Mombasa site reportedly cut Uganda latency from about 150 ms to about 20 ms ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com:2087/mombasa-kenya-cloudflares-43rd-data-center)).

## Hosting stays free on Cloudflare, and Netlify's cap is the trap

Cloudflare is the recommended host. Static asset requests are free and unlimited ([Static assets billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/billing-and-limitations/)). The notes' estimate is that the R2-plus-cache path stays free at 1,000 and 10,000 daily visitors and probably needs only a few dollars at 100,000. That estimate assumes about 0.2 MB and 7 requests per visit. Visitors who leave the page open will exceed it, since a 20-second poll over 30 minutes is 90 requests.

Netlify is the fallback, not the first choice. Its Scheduled Functions are available on every plan with a 30-second limit, but cron gives a 60-second floor. The free plan is a **300-credit hard cap**, about 15 GB of bandwidth, and the site pauses when credits run out ([Netlify pricing](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/); [How credits work](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/how-credits-work/)). By the notes' estimate, 1,000 visitors a day uses about 162 credits and 10,000 a day uses about 1,620, roughly five times the free pool. If the builder already uses Netlify, the practical compromise is to keep static HTML there and point `fetch()` at the Cloudflare JSON domain with CORS headers. That moves the heavy traffic to Cloudflare's free egress. Vercel Hobby is out because its cron runs only once a day ([Vercel cron](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing)). Render's free tier sleeps after 15 minutes idle, which kills a poller ([Render free docs](https://render.com/docs/free)). A Hetzner VPS allows true 15-second polling and costs about EUR 5.49 per month from June 2026, though that price comes from a secondary source ([privatedevops.com](https://privatedevops.com/news/hetzner-june-2026-cloud-price-increase-what-to-do)). It is a later option for a builder with Linux skills, not a zero-budget start.

## Licensing is the real cost, and scraping does not avoid it

No provider's terms could be read, so permission for public display, caching and ads is unverified for API-Sports, Goalserve and Sportmonks. The only hint for API-Football is a competitor's claim that its terms allow commercial use on all tiers, including free ([TheStatsAPI](https://thestatsapi.com/blog/free-football-api-alternatives)). Treat it as unconfirmed. The terms that were visible show what to look for. Goalserve permits displaying data in your application but not reselling raw feeds ([Goalserve llms.txt](https://www.goalserve.com/llms.txt)). SportsDataIO requires a commercial licence for public products and attribution, and its free tier is personal and non-commercial only ([SportsDataIO data rights](https://sportsdata.io/help/data-rights-and-licensing-questions)). Sportradar's B2B feeds are meant to be called from a backend, with rights set by contract ([SportsAPIs.dev](https://sportsapis.dev/apis/sportradar)). The safe pattern is to keep the key server-side, cache briefly, show any required attribution, and never publish a raw mirror of the feed. Treat team logos and player photos as a separate licensing question, because rights over them can differ from rights over scores.

Scraping is a bad foundation. Flashscore's terms, under Livesport and Maltese law, forbid copying, downloading, distributing or uploading content without written authorisation, and reserve the right to block visitors and seek damages ([Flashscore terms](https://livesport.eu/terms/flashscore_ae)). A public, ad-supported site is far more likely to attract a claim than a private prototype. The feed also breaks whenever the target changes its markup or blocks the IP, and it carries no licence to the underlying league data. That Apify scrapers exist shows demand, not legality ([Apify listing](https://apify.com/superapis/flashscore)). The notes did not retrieve case law or any Ugandan or EU/Malta legal analysis, so none of this is legal advice. The cheaper lawful path is to prototype in non-commercial mode on free tiers, get written permission before the public launch, and launch with football only.

## What to test first, in this order

The first test costs nothing and decides everything. Create a free API-Football key and call `/leagues?country=Uganda` and `/leagues?country=World`, then look for the Uganda Premier League, CAF Champions League, CAF Confederation Cup and AFCON. If the Uganda Premier League is missing, the product's main local draw is missing. The notes found no source confirming Ugandan or CAF coverage for any provider, only that API-Football claims 1,200+ leagues with "uneven" quality outside the top five European leagues ([TheStatsAPI](https://thestatsapi.com/blog/best-football-api)). Repeat the check with the Goalserve 30-day trial and the Sportmonks 14-day trial (which needs a credit card) before paying anyone.

Second, check whether data actually arrives for small leagues in time. One report says some competitions show delays despite the 15-second claim ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/checklive/navigating-the-challenges-of-free-sports-data-apis-football-basketball-and-baseball-insights-3gok)). Compare goal timestamps against a TV broadcast or an official site on a real Uganda Premier League match. Third, find out which sports API-Sports actually sells. Confirm on the dashboard whether volleyball and basketball are separate subscriptions with separate quotas, what the per-minute limit is (no number was found), and whether any tennis product exists. Fourth, email the chosen vendor for written confirmation of public website display, server-side caching, ads on the page and logo or attribution rules. Fifth, test the hosting assumptions: that a Cron Worker can run at once a minute within free limits, that the Cache Everything rule serves R2 JSON without invoking a Worker, and what the custom domain costs. Also measure real latency from Kampala on mobile data, since none of the notes has a measured figure. Last, check domestic volleyball league coverage (Italy, Poland, Brazil, Turkey), which no source confirms.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Data cost is not the barrier for football: $19 per month covers a live, cached, many-visitor football site. The barriers are coverage of the one league that makes the site relevant in Uganda, the missing licence text, and tennis. A realistic plan is to launch football first on API-Football, add basketball and volleyball only after the dashboard check, and treat tennis as a paid milestone funded by traffic or sponsorship. Goalserve's $150 trial is useful in the meantime for building and testing a tennis parser at no cost.

The architecture is the stable part. Because upstream calls scale with sports and not visitors, switching providers or adding a second one changes only the poller's adapter. That is why the normalized status model and the provider-agnostic JSON files matter more than the first provider choice. The cheapest mistake to avoid is paying for any plan before the Uganda and CAF league check and the written-permission email are done, since both can invalidate the provider choice.
